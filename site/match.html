Read this first: compliance

  • Duty depends on two things: the product's HTS classification and its true country of origin (where it was wholly obtained or substantially transformed, under the program's rules of origin).
  • A supplier's nonprofit status, tax breaks, or debts do not change US duty. Neither does how you pay (crypto, SPACE, or dollars). CBP does not accept crypto for duties.
  • Transshipment (routing goods through a program country to dodge duty) and misdeclaring origin is customs fraud (19 U.S.C. 1592; criminal penalties possible).
  • To claim a preference you need the program's certification / certificate of origin and records to back it up. Keep them for at least 5 years.
  • Additional Chapter 99 duties (e.g. Section 232 / 301 and other actions) may still apply on top of, or despite, a preference. Check the HTS.

The programs

  • USMCA (Canada, Mexico; HTS codes S, S+). Goods must meet USMCA rules of origin.
  • Other US FTAs: Australia (AU), Bahrain (BH), Chile (CL), Colombia (CO), CAFTA-DR: Costa Rica, Dominican Rep., El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua (P, P+), Israel (IL), Jordan (JO), Korea (KR), Morocco (MA), Oman (OM), Panama (PA), Peru (PE), Singapore (SG).
  • GSP (A, A*, A+): dark. Authorization lapsed Dec 31, 2020 and Congress has not renewed it. Duties are paid at the normal rate; the HTS still lists GSP codes.
  • AGOA (D): lit. Reauthorized (P.L. 119-75, Feb 2026; extended by P.L. 119-103) through Dec 31, 2028 for eligible sub-Saharan African countries. Eligibility is set yearly; check the current list.
  • Caribbean Basin (CBERA/CBTPA) (E, R): check HTS General Note 7/17 and current eligibility.
  • Foreign-trade zones: not a country preference. Goods in an FTZ can defer duty, and you may choose the duty rate of the finished product versus its components (rules apply). See FTZ Board.
  • Duty drawback: refund of up to 99% of duties paid on imports that are later exported or destroyed (19 U.S.C. 1313). See CBP drawback.
